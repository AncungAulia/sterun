Event console, revision 5

Half rings for the distances, a smooth area chart for the daily entries, and every panel title cut down to a plain noun: Distances, Activity, Entries per day, Add-ons. The explanatory line under each one is gone, because it described how the thing worked, which is the sort of copy a runner or an organiser never needs.

The add-ons panel no longer assumes jerseys: the contract stores a code, a quota and a reserved count per add-on, so a tumbler or a finisher medal is the same kind of row. Block 4 holds the bar version of Distances, so that comparison still exists in reverse.

Dashboard

Not a smaller copy of the event page. This one answers a different question: which race needs me today. So the list of what is waiting sits at the top, where the eye lands, and the three totals are a slim strip at the bottom, because a total is the one thing here nobody can act on.

Each race also carries fourteen days of entries next to its bar. A bar says 312 of 500; the line says whether it is still moving. Jogja Night Run's line falls to the floor because its race already happened, and Borobudur Trial's is flat on zero because entries have never been opened.

Pace is the comparison you asked for, and the trick is the axis: it counts days to race day, not calendar dates, so races that ran months apart lie on top of each other. The vertical is percent of quota, because 240 places and 500 places are not the same race. Your finished race is the dashed grey line, and it is the only honest benchmark you have. Read it and Fun Run Sleman tells you something no table on this page can: three days out it is 29% full, where your last race was at 94%. That is why it is at the top of Needs you.

The Scanners tab

Shaped like Entries: search and the one action sit above the card, the card holds nothing but the table. No stat cards, because three numbers about three rows would be furniture. The address is the whole of what the contract stores, so the table is a wallet, a date and a way out. An address can only be registered from here, which is why this tab has to exist before the scanner app does.

On a phone

390px, which is where this console is actually used on the morning of a race. The sidebar folds into the button top left, the tabs scroll sideways, and every panel goes full width in one column. Two things carried over on purpose: the tabs stay visible, because Scanners is the tab someone opens standing at the gate, and the half rings keep their key underneath rather than beside them. The right-hand frame is the warning state, which is the whole reason the Dashboard flags a missing scanner three days out.

The bar version, for comparison

your call

Same three numbers. The one thing to check with your eyes: on the bars every distance starts from the same left edge, so 21K being nearly empty is visible before you read a single number. On the half rings each arc curves at its own radius, so the shortest one has the smallest circle to travel and looks fuller than it is. Pick whichever you would rather look at every day.
