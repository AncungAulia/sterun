The registration close date, in the console (STE-69)

The contract has enforced a close date since this morning (STE-46, spec v2.5.0): past it, enter refuses with RegistrationClosed(20) even while the race's status is still Open. The web app calls none of it. The wizard writes the date into the event document, which runners read, and nothing puts it on chain, so today a race page can promise a date the contract will ignore.

This is what closes that gap: the wizard sets what it publishes, the console shows the date and can move it, and a runner who arrives late is told which of the two refusals they met.

One rule the chain cannot enforce, and this screen must. Moving the date is a real change to what runners were promised, so it is paired with a signed announcement, exactly as raising a quota is (STE-57). Never write it up as though the contract checks that.

The menu restores a rule rather than breaking one

`ConsoleHeader` holds one action, and STE-57 already broke that on purpose to keep Close entries reachable. A third button would have made the exception the rule. A menu is one button, so the header is back to one thing, and the other two live where a second-tier action belongs.

One line per item, with a mark of its own rather than a sentence under it (Ancung, 2026-09-27): a menu is a list of things to do, and a paragraph under each turns choosing into reading. The marks are what tell two items that both stop entries apart before the words are read.

The number keeps its card: it is a fact rather than an action, and it reads beside the other date an organiser thinks about, race day.

What the picker refuses

The same DateTimeField the create-race wizard uses, with the month and year dropdowns it already has. Its bounds come from the race itself: a closing date may not sit after race pack collection starts where the document names one, and never after race day.

Handing packs out while people can still enter means a runner paying for a race whose pack has already gone out. The document's race pack window is optional, so where it is missing, race day is the bound.

Why this is allowed at all

Because an organiser can already stop entries this second with Close entries. Moving the date earlier adds no power to harm a runner; it only makes the stop automatic and announced.

What it does take away is days somebody was promised, which is why the announcement is not optional here either. Extending gives; bringing forward takes.

The sentence never says "extended". It names both dates and lets the reader see which direction they moved.

SCREEN 3

Reopen entries has to stop lying

Today the header offers Reopen entries on a Closed race and says nothing about dates. Past the close date that button changes the status and opens nothing, which is the worst kind of button: it succeeds and does not work.

Reopen entries?

This alone will not let anyone enter.

Entries for this race closed on their own on 12 October, and that date is what the contract checks. Reopening the status leaves them closed until the date moves.

You can do both: reopen the status, then set a new closing date. Runners are told about the new date.

Screen 3. The main button is the one that actually works, and the other one is still there because the organiser may want the status right first.

Why not just hide Reopen

Because the status is real and an organiser may genuinely want it back to Open before choosing a date, for instance while they decide. Hiding a control that works, to prevent a misunderstanding a sentence can prevent, trades one confusion for another.

On a race with no date this dialog is exactly what it is today. The warning appears only where it is true, which is the same rule the refund notice follows.

One more line on an open race

The third card is the ordinary state, with the closing date under the button. It comes from the chain, not from the document, so it is the date that will actually be enforced. Where the two disagree, a runner deserves the one that decides.

The race page keeps showing the document's own registration window in Timeline, unchanged: that is what was promised when the race was published, and an extension is a new fact beside it rather than a rewrite of it.

Neither card explains itself further. "Still on, registration is simply over" is a sentence the date already says.
