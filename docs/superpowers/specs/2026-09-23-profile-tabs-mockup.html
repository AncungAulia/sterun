/profile, with a face and three tabs

What the page holds does not change: the wallet, the test money, the races this wallet is in, and the race record. What changes is that the three sections stop being one long scroll, and that the wallet gets a picture instead of only 56 characters.

The blobatar is drawn from the address, so it is a fact rather than a decoration: the same wallet is the same creature on every device, and on /runner/[address] too. It is the quickest way to see you are looking at the right wallet, which is the thing 56 base32 characters are worst at.

What was built and what was not. The tabs were built, with the faucet last and the tab in the address, and the strip ended up divided evenly across the full width with a history icon on the record and the word Faucet rather than "Test money". The blobatar was built and then removed: on the real page, beside one address, it added nothing, and "See what others see" went with it because the record tab already carries "Open the public page". This file is kept as the record of that, not as a picture of the page.

One change to the order asked for. Test money is last, not first. It only exists on testnet, so on mainnet that tab is not there at all and a page whose first tab disappears opens somewhere else depending on the network. And a runner opens this page for their pass, at a desk, on race morning. Entries first.

Why the picture earns its place

A wallet address is the one thing on this page a person cannot read back to themselves. Two addresses that differ in the middle look identical at a glance, and the shortened form in the header hides exactly the part that differs.

A blobatar is a pure function of the address: same wallet, same creature, on this laptop, on the phone at the start line, and on the public record somebody else opens. Nothing is stored and nothing is asked for.

Four different wallets, four different creatures:

In the app it is rendered on the device, never fetched from blobatar.dev: a URL carrying the address would hand every runner's wallet to somebody else's server on every page view. Only this mockup uses the endpoint.

Why tabs are right here and wrong on the directory

Three sections, each of which answers a different question, and nobody needs two of them at once: the pass is for a desk, the record is for showing somebody, the faucet is for the first five minutes of a testnet account.

A scroll made the page's length depend on how many races you have run, so on a phone the faucet sat below eleven record cards.

What tabs would cost on a page where the parts are compared, like the directory, is the comparison itself. Here there is none to lose.

The tab is in the address

/profile?tab=entries, the same rule the race console already follows, so a pass is one link away from a chat message and Back does what it says.
