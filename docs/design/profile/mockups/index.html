Runner profile

STE-23, the design STE-24 is built from. A public page at /runner/<address> that reads a runner's whole race history straight from the contract, with no wallet, no login and no account. Twelve artboards: the page, a record in every state it can be in, the block that proves a record belongs to a person, and the four screens where there is nothing to list.

The page

A record is a card, not a table row. A table at 390px either scrolls sideways or shrinks the finish time to nothing, and the finish time is the reason anybody opened this page. The address is the heading because that is what the URL is; a name would be a claim this page cannot check.

One record, seven ways

Four states live on the chain, but they read as seven different things to a runner. The two that matter most are the pairs that look identical in the data and mean opposite things to a person.

Proving a record belongs to a person

This is the most dangerous block in the product, because a public page asking for a national ID is exactly what a phishing page looks like. Saying "nothing is sent anywhere" is not enough; people do not believe sentences. The design has to make it observable: the hash forms on screen as you type, the button says what it sends, and the only thing that ever leaves the page is 32 bytes that reveal nothing.

When there is nothing to list

Four different reasons for an empty page, and they are not interchangeable. Three of them are not the visitor's fault and one of them is a typo, so each gets its own sentence and its own way out.
